# Common Mistakes and Their Corrections

Six errors account for most bad JTBD work. When a diagnosis feels wrong,
check this list.

## 1. Confusing jobs with activities

"Schedule a meeting" is an activity, not a job. The job is the underlying
progress: "ensure stakeholders are aligned before a critical decision gets
made without them."

**Correction:** activities are what people do. Jobs are the progress they
seek. Keep asking why until the answer stops changing.

## 2. Defining jobs as features

A job that depends on a specific technology ("upload a file to the cloud")
is a solution description. If the solution vanished, the job must survive.

**Correction:** strip the technology, keep the verb and object. See the
transformation tables in [formats](formats.md).

## 3. Ignoring the emotional dimension

Teams that map only functional outcomes build features nobody feels loyalty
toward. The functional job gets you into the market. The emotional and social
jobs build attachment.

**Correction:** every job statement passes the three-dimension check:
functional, emotional, social.

## 4. Conflating personas with jobs

Wildly different personas do the same job. A CEO and a teenager can
both hire a ride-share to get somewhere. The job is the unit of analysis.

**Correction:** segment by job and situation first, persona second. When two
personas share a job, one analysis covers both.

## 5. Treating JTBD as a one-time exercise

Jobs are stable, but the solutions people hire change. A workshop deliverable
ages into fiction once the market moves.

**Correction:** treat JTBD as an ongoing listening practice. Revisit the
analysis when the solution landscape shifts.

## 6. Keeping the four forces abstract

Forces mapped as categories ("dissatisfaction", "benefits", "fear",
"inertia") are decoration, not analysis.

**Correction:** ground every force in a quoted moment from a real
person's story. If you cannot quote the anxiety, you have not found it yet.

## Quick self-check

Before delivering any analysis:

- Does any job statement name a solution? (Rules 1, 2)
- Did you check all three dimensions? (Rule 3)
- Are you segmenting by persona when you should segment by job? (Rule 4)
- Is the forces map built from quoted evidence? (Rule 6)
