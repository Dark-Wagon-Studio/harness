# The Universal Job Map and Desired Outcomes (ODI)

The ODI school (Ulwick) deconstructs any job into a stable, universal
process, then measures which steps are underserved.

## The eight universal steps

1. **Define**: determine objectives and plan the approach.
2. **Locate**: gather the necessary inputs and information.
3. **Prepare**: organize and set up the environment.
4. **Confirm**: verify that everything is ready.
5. **Execute**: perform the core task.
6. **Monitor**: assess performance while the executor does the job.
7. **Modify**: make adjustments when something goes wrong.
8. **Conclude**: finish the job and evaluate the result.

For each step, generate 5–10 desired outcome statements. These are the
metrics the customer uses to judge success at that step.

## Desired outcome statement syntax

> **Minimize / Increase** + [performance metric] + [object of action] +
> [contextual clarifier]

Examples:

- "Minimize the time it takes to locate a specific document when working
  remotely."
- "Increase the likelihood that meeting attendees arrive prepared before the
  call begins."
- "Minimize the likelihood of introducing errors when re-keying data between
  systems."

## The four rules for ODI outcomes

1. **Functional**: not emotional.
2. **Stable**: valid for years.
3. **Solution-independent**: no products, technologies, or features.
4. **Measurable**: time, likelihood, or number.

## Valid/invalid transformation table

| Invalid (contaminated) | Valid (ODI outcome) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Use the search bar to find files fast" | "Minimize the time it takes to locate a needed file" | Names a solution ("search bar") |
| "Make the app feel reliable" | "Increase the likelihood that a saved change persists across sessions" | "Feel reliable" is unmeasurable |
| "Sync data via the cloud" | "Minimize the time it takes to make new data available on all devices" | Technology named instead of progress measured |

## Measuring: importance and satisfaction

Survey customers who do the job. For each outcome statement, collect:

- **Importance** (1–10): how much it matters.
- **Satisfaction** (1–10): how well current solutions deliver it.

Only survey data produces ratings. Never convert qualitative text into
numbers. An outcome without ratings does not get a score.

## Opportunity score

> **Opportunity = Importance + max(Importance − Satisfaction, 0)**

High importance with low satisfaction is a high-value, underserved
opportunity. An outcome that is already well served (satisfaction ≥
importance) carries no extra opportunity regardless of its importance. Rank
outcomes by opportunity score to prioritize investment. Use the
[score sheet](../assets/opportunity-score-sheet.md) when the user supplies
ratings.
