# The Two Schools of JTBD

JTBD is a theory, not a single method. Two schools of practice grew from it.
A coach must know both and apply the right one to the decision at hand.

## The Christensen / Moesta / Spiek school (demand-side)

- **Origins.** Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek at the Re-Wired Group
  co-developed it. Clayton Christensen at Harvard Business School popularized
  it.
- **Focus.** Understanding *why* people buy and switch. Qualitative,
  narrative, demand-side.
- **Primary tool.** The **switch interview**: a retrospective, 45–90 minute
  interview reconstructing the buyer's story of moving from one solution to
  another. The goal is not features. The goal is the struggling moment that
  created demand.
- **Key insight.** Switching is not rational optimization. A struggle
  triggers it, and the struggle makes the current way unacceptable.

## The Ulwick / ODI school (outcome-driven)

- **Origins.** Tony Ulwick developed it at IBM in the 1980s. It evolved into
  **Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI)**.
- **Focus.** Defining the complete set of customer needs (desired outcomes),
  mapping them to a job process, and quantifying which needs are underserved
  with survey data.
- **Primary tool.** The **universal job map** and **desired outcome
  statements**. Quantitative methods identify the highest-value opportunities
  before anyone builds anything.
- **Key insight.** Customer needs decompose into stable, measurable,
  solution-agnostic desired outcomes that stay valid for years.

## How to choose

- **Demand-side school** for demand generation, why people switch, sales and
  messaging work, and the emotional or psychological drivers of a decision.
- **ODI** for multi-million-dollar investment decisions, roadmap
  prioritization against quantified opportunity gaps, and complete
  addressable market definition around a job.

Most mature teams use both. Switch interviews uncover the "why" behind
demand. ODI maps the specific gaps worth closing.

## The Klement tension

Alan Klement (job stories, popularized via Intercom) argues jobs are
primarily emotional and that desired outcomes cannot be reliably quantified.
This is a real disagreement with the ODI school. Pragmatic resolution: the
Klement format is excellent for feature design and discovery. The ODI format
is superior for investment prioritization and market definition. Both are
valid. Present the right tool for the right job.
