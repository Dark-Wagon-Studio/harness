# The Switch Interview

The primary tool of the demand-side school (Moesta / Re-Wired Group). A
retrospective, 45–90 minute interview that reconstructs one real switching
event. You do not collect opinions. You recover a timeline.

## Method

1. **Pick one real switch.** The person must have actually changed
   solutions, by buying, adopting, or abandoning, within a period they
   remember.
2. **Reconstruct the timeline backwards.** Start at the switch, walk back to
   the first thought that something had to change.
3. **Collect the four forces as they lived.** What pushed, what pulled, what
   scared, what held. In the person's own words, with dates and context.
4. **Find the struggling moment.** The point where the old way stopped being
   acceptable. Probe what was happening in their life around it.
5. **Map compensating behaviors.** What did they do before the switch, and
   what did it cost them? Spreadsheets, duct tape, and doing nothing all
   count.

## Question bank

Non-leading, past-tense, one at a time:

- Walk me back to the day you first decided something had to change.
- What happened in your life right before that?
- What happened at work or at home around that time?
- What did you try first? What happened?
- Tell me about the last time you used the old solution. What made that day
  different?
- What almost stopped you from switching?
- What did you think might go wrong with the new option?
- Who else was involved in the decision? What did they think?
- What did you have to give up or set up to make the change?
- When did you first hear about the new option? What did you think then?
- What had to be true before you were willing to switch?
- If the new solution disappeared tomorrow, what would you do?

## What to listen for

- **Workarounds**: spreadsheets, scripts, manual re-entry. Signs of an
  underserved job.
- **Compensating behaviors**: habits that absorb the pain and delay the
  switch.
- **The struggling moment**: often social or emotional, not functional.
  Quote it verbatim.
- **Dates and sequence**: the order of events is the causal story. Vague
  "it was annoying" means you have not found the moment yet.

## Forbidden questions

- Leading feature questions: "Wouldn't it be great if it did X?"
- Future speculation: "Would you use Y?" / "How likely are you to...?"
- Opinion questions: "What do you think of our product?"
- Solution pitching of any kind, at any point.

People are unreliable witnesses about the future and the hypothetical. They
are reliable about the recent, concrete past. Ask about that.

## Output

The interview yields: the timeline, the struggling moment (quoted), the four
forces as lived, and findings ranked by evidence level (direct evidence,
inference, or unverified claim).
Stage the result on the readiness ladder and name the smallest next
validation step. If the transcript never establishes both the current and the
prospective solution, the forces analysis stops. See the stopping rules in
[SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).
