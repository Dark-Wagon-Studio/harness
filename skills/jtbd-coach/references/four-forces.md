# The Four Forces of Progress

The core demand-side model (Christensen / Moesta). Every switching decision
is a tug-of-war between four forces. Two push toward change. Two resist it.

| Force | Direction | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Push of the situation** | Away from current solution | Frustration, pain, or struggling moment that makes the current state untenable |
| **Pull of the new solution** | Toward the new solution | The progress the new option promises, not its feature list |
| **Anxiety of the new solution** | Away from new solution | Fear, uncertainty, and risk of switching. The hidden conversion killer |
| **Habit of the present** | Toward current solution | Comfort, inertia, familiarity of the current way |

## The switch rule

**Push + Pull must exceed Anxiety + Habit.** Two ways to cause a switch:

1. Amplify the top forces: make the struggle acute, make the promised
   progress vivid.
2. Weaken the bottom forces: reduce anxiety, ease the transition.

Innovation is often less about a better product and more about removing the
friction of switching.

## The struggling moment

A struggling moment creates demand. It is the instant a person realizes the
current way of doing things is no longer acceptable. Before it, no demand
exists. People ignore the marketing and reject the sales outreach. The struggle is often social,
emotional, or situational rather than functional. If you cannot identify a
specific, vivid struggle, the market does not exist yet. Say so.

## Coaching questions

- What is the specific anxiety holding this person back from switching?
- What habit anchors them to the status quo?
- What happened in their life when the old way stopped being good enough?
- What would they have to fire to make room for this?

## Applied example

Context: empty-nesters weighing a move from a large house to a condo.

Valid application (each force grounded in the person's story):

- **Push:** "The house is too big, the kids moved out, the laundry is in the
  basement."
- **Pull:** "First-floor living, an updated kitchen, safety in a condo."
- **Anxiety:** "Will I lose my privacy? Can I afford the HOA fees?"
- **Habit:** "I know this neighborhood. I have memories here."

Invalid application (abstract, no story): "Push: dissatisfaction with the
home. Pull: condo benefits. Anxiety: change. Habit: attachment." If you
cannot quote the person struggling, you do not understand the bottom forces
yet. Map forces against real stories, not categories.
