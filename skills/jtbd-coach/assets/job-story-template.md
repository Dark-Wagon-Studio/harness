# Job Story Template

Fill one block per story. Situations select behavior. Spend your effort
there.

The worked examples below are synthetic. They show the format. They are not
research data.

## Blank scaffold

```
When [specific situation],
I want to [motivation, the progress sought],
so I can [expected outcome].

Functional dimension: [the practical task]
Emotional dimension: [how they want to feel]
Social dimension:    [how they want to be seen]
Evidence:            [direct quote / candidate inference / unverified claim]
```

Field notes:

- **Situation.** Concrete and time-bound ("when I have 10 minutes between
  tasks"), not a demographic ("as a busy professional").
- **Motivation.** The progress, never the feature. "Skip the line", not
  "a mobile ordering app".
- **Outcome.** What changes for the person, including how they feel or are
  seen, not what the UI does.
- **Evidence.** Cite the quote that supports the story, or flag the whole
  story `candidate`.

## Worked example 1: validated (synthetic)

> When I am running early to a client meeting, I want to skip the coffee
> shop line, so I can arrive on time and feel composed.
>
> Functional: get coffee without waiting.
> Emotional: feel composed, not rushed.
> Social: be the person who arrives on time.
> Evidence (direct): "If I'm late again I look like I can't manage my own
> calendar." (synthetic example, not real research)

## Worked example 2: candidate (synthetic)

> When a pull request sits unreviewed for two days, I want a way to nudge
> reviewers without sounding naggy, so I can keep the release moving and
> stay on good terms with the team.
>
> Functional: get reviews completed promptly.
> Emotional: feel in control of the release. Avoid anxiety about bothering
> people.
> Social: be seen as a strong coordinator, not a nag.
> Evidence (candidate): inferred from forum complaints. No interviews yet.
  Next step: two switch interviews with engineers who changed review tools.
